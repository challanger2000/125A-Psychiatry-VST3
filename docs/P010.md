# P010 — actual VST3 wet/dry, effect distinctiveness and anti-placebo test

**2026-11-23** · Tested source **`ceb57ac2dc4d16b274cd2efcec2200f2d9b37e5a`** on `main` · VST3 factory version remains **0.9.0** (no processor changes in P010).

## User's binding audio contract

| Control | 0% | 100% |
|---|---|---|
| Any of the nine effect macros | **100% original Dry, 0% FX** | **100% processed FX output, 0% additional Dry mixer path** |
| Global THERAPY | **Fully Wet**, selected effects applied | **Original Dry** |

Intermediate values are mixed according to their macro algorithm, with higher macro levels capable of increasing the internal timbral effect intensity. THERAPY is an inverse overall Wet/Dry crossfade, evaluated after all nine engines.

**Important precision:** *100% Wet* means the VST3 applies no extra, independent bypass/dry crossfade to the engine's processed signal. Because it is an input-driven complex FX, the wet engine may still contain a pitch-shifted, nonlinear, reverberated, transformed or otherwise source-dependent branch. It does not mean an arbitrary FX has zero time-aligned original spectral content by mathematical coincidence.

## Method — real `Processor::process`

`tests/test_vst3_audio_path.cpp` instantiates the actual C++ VST3 processor, calls `setupProcessing` with **48 kHz, stereo, float32, 256-sample blocks**, loads the genuine **v4 processor state** through Steinberg's `IBStream`, activates and runs real `Vst::ProcessData` blocks, and collects output samples for all comparisons. It is not a direct call to the DSP alone. DSP direct calls are used **only as reference signals** at the full-Wet endpoint.

Sources (each **60,000 samples = 1.25 seconds**):
1. Synth bass: harmonic notes with amplitude decay.
2. Percussion: kick, snare noise and hat-like transients.
3. Guitar-like: harmonically rich chopped distorted synthetic riff.
4. Voice-like: band-limited vowel-formant harmonic phrases; **not a human vocal recording**.
5. Cinematic atmosphere: low oscillators, slowly changing air/noise.

Per source, each of nine effects is independently set to **0%, 20%, 50%, 75%, 100%** while the other eight are off and THERAPY remains 0%. **180 measurement rows** (5 × 9 × 4 nonzero positions) are exported to CSV. The zero positions and bypass are validated using **sample-identical** rather than RMS thresholds.

### Explicit evidence and negative controls

- **0% exact original**: the real VST3's output matches the input sample-for-sample for each inactive macro. If all nine are zero, it also remains bit-exact, even with global THERAPY=0.
- **100% Wet**: actual VST3 output compared with the same raw, fully wet DSP class rendering the identical input. Tolerance **3×10⁻⁶ maximum absolute error**, across *all 45 effect/source combinations*. PASS: demonstrates no additional crossfade with original Dry at full macro.
- **THERAPY 100%**: exact input when any one of nine macros is 100%. **THERAPY 50%**: sample-accurate `dry + 0.5×(fully_wet−dry)` within **5×10⁻⁶** (synth bass × each macro). **Bypass**: exact Dry.
- **Anti-placebo reference**: 0.41× input waveform is deliberately modified in volume but leaves **gain-matched residual nearly zero** and normalized spectral bands unchanged. The test must reject any case equivalent merely to this volume change. Actual plugins are judged on *gain-matched waveform residual*, not volume change alone.
- **Full-Wet effect gate:** `gainMatchedResidual >= 0.035` and `rawNullRatio >= 0.06`. PASS **45/45** full-Wet macro/source combinations; the **weakest** normalized waveform residual was ~**0.328** for PANIC on synthetic guitar. Residual near 1 indicates highly dissimilar processed content, *not necessarily better sound quality*.
- **Four spectral-band normalized energy descriptors**: bands approximating below 140 Hz, 140–900 Hz, 900–3600 Hz and above 3600 Hz from cascaded one-pole response; exported as `band_shape_distance` and checked against the pure-gain negative control. These are not a full FFT or a high-resolution aliasing test.
- **Sample-accurate automation:** real Steinberg `ParameterChanges` queue switches MANIA from 0 to 1 at exactly **sample offset 128**. The first 128 samples are bit-exact Dry; the remainder differs after event application.

Full CSV columns: `source,macro,amount,therapy,dry_rms,output_rms,gain_db,peak_dbfs,raw_null_ratio,gain_matched_residual,band_shape_distance`.

## Conclusions, including remaining concerns

**Measured:** All nine effects have *real* output consequences. The tested full-Wet state is not a dummy knob, an untouched source passed through unchanged, or merely gain. The zero, full-Wet, THERAPY and bypass endpoints are functioning correctly in the actual Windows VST3 signal path.

**Not yet measured:** genuine music/vocal recordings, whether effects are *desirable* rather than just different, spectral aliasing above Nyquist, acoustic clicks from reverse/grain capture, latency/tempo correctness during full DAW play, and CPU stability with many instances in Studio Pro.

In particular, **full-Wet DISSOCIATION on synthetic drums is ~−11.12 dB RMS** relative to Dry, while **HALLUCINATION on synth bass is ~−7.46 dB**, and **PANIC voice-like +4.47 dB**. These are not placebo effects, but differing levels can mask musical usefulness or produce unexpected mix balance. Treat them as follow-up gain-staging review rather than falsely claiming a final sound sign-off.
